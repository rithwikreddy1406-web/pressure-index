What the data says

Read these before the rankings. They set how much weight each list deserves.

A small robust core

Klaasen, Suryakumar Yadav and Tim David (batters) and Narine (bowlers) have a 75% or higher chance of a top-10 place. Shanaka and Rashid Khan are above 60%. Every other place is a borderline contest (31% to 58%).

Pressure-specific talent is hard to isolate

Between-player spread under pressure is about 2.4 times what shuffled data produces for batters and only 1.2 times for bowlers. Random balls show similar spread, so much of what we measure is general ability. The change versus a player's own ordinary level carries almost no between-player signal.

Clutch labels are not supported beyond chance

At 90% intervals, 5 of 132 batters and 4 of 143 bowlers are labelled clutch, and 1 and 7 pressure-sensitive. That is about what chance alone would produce, so treat each label as a hypothesis.

Rajasthan Royals are mid-table

RR rank 3rd of 10 franchises in batting under pressure on the primary definition (1st on PI, 7th on chase and collapse rules) and 4th to 5th of 10 in bowling. "RR cannot chase" is not supported: 19 of 36 chases were won against 52% league-wide. The best additions under the four-overseas rule are modest and uncertain: about 1.1 runs a match at 50% persistence (90% interval 0.1 to 2.3). This is a ranking device, not a forecast.

Squad optimiser

  
For each candidate, the modelled gain per match is the pressure balls he would face or bowl, times his hierarchical value minus RR's own average value per ball in the same situation, times a persistence factor (25% conservative, 50% base, 75% optimistic). Combinations use at most two batters and two bowlers, at most four overseas players, and at most 38 RR bowling balls a match. Shanaka and Jaiswal already play for RR, and Pakistani players such as Rauf have not played in the IPL since 2008, so none of the three is treated as an addition. These are model-implied estimates against RR's average ball, not forecasts.

Clutch classification

  

  
Clutch if the whole 90% interval of the pressure-minus-ordinary difference is above zero, pressure-sensitive if below zero, consistent if the interval includes zero and both values are above zero. Insufficient evidence applies below 300 ordinary balls for batters or 150 for bowlers, or when the interval is too wide.

Pressure momentum

  
For every delivery in the 785 usable matches, the model gives the win probability before the ball, the Pressure Index and the change in win probability. Estimated impact is not proven causation: a change in win probability credits the participants on the ball and does not separate skill from context, opposition or luck.

Why the hierarchical model replaced empirical Bayes

  
Leave-one-season-out (5 folds) and leave-one-opposition-team-out (12 folds) validation. Lower error is better. The hierarchical model beat empirical Bayes in every season fold and in 10 of 12 (batters) and 7 of 12 (bowlers) team folds, with paired-bootstrap intervals that exclude zero. Almost all of the gain comes from context terms: adding the player effect improves held-out error by only 0.001 for batters and 0.0004 for bowlers.

  

  

    
Is pressure ability separate from general ability?

      
Between-player spread of the pressure effect, with two negative controls (lower bars). Spread should exceed the shuffled null if real player differences exist, and the random-flag value if pressure-specific differences exist.

      

      
For batters there is a detectable player effect but it is about the size seen on random balls, so it is mostly general ability. For bowlers the real effect barely exceeds the shuffled null.

    
Does the rating persist?

      
Built on 2022–24, tested on 2025–26.

      
  • Rank correlation with later seasons: +0.03 to +0.08 for batters (intervals include zero) and +0.20 to +0.30 for bowlers, similar to naive strike rate and economy.
    • 
      
  • Split-half agreement of the composite: +0.23 (batters) and +0.14 (bowlers), against +0.42 and +0.31 for naive statistics. Naive statistics partly reflect role, which the adjusted measure removes.

  

  
Ablation: what each component does

  
Each component is removed in turn. Rank correlation and top-10 overlap are measured against the full model. Shrinkage matters most for the ranking, consistency matters for bowlers, and the vs-usual comparison contributes almost nothing and keeps a small weight. Differences in later-season correlation are within sampling noise.

Method

  
A gradient-boosting classifier gives the batting side's win probability from any ball state, fitted separately for each innings with 5-fold cross-validation grouped by match and isotonic recalibration. The Pressure Index is the expected absolute win-probability swing of a ball, divided by the average over all balls, so 1 is an average ball and 3 has three times the stakes.

Definitions and weights

      
    
        
  • High pressure: PI of 1.5 or more (22.2% of balls) or a rule flag: a chase needing 10 an over with 12 or fewer overs left, 9 an over with 4+ wickets down, or a collapse of 3+ wickets in the last 24 balls. For bowlers, a death over with the chasing side's win probability at 20–80%, or a first innings against a side with 5+ wickets in hand. The union is 38.5% of balls faced and 29.6% of deliveries.
    • 
        
  • Intermediate: PI from 1.0 to 1.5 with no rule flag, excluded from both groups in all 48 specifications.
    • 
        
  • Ordinary: PI below 1 and no rule flag.
    • 
        
  • Composite: pressure level, change versus own ordinary level, win-probability impact, consistency and breadth. Weight set A is 0.45 / 0.10 / 0.25 / 0.12 / 0.08, set B is 0.40 / 0.05 / 0.30 / 0.15 / 0.10.
    • 
        
  • Eligibility: batters 150+ pressure balls, 12+ innings and median batting position 7 or higher. Bowlers 90+ pressure balls and 10+ spells.
    • 
        
  • Specifications: 3 pressure definitions × 4 wicket costs × 2 opposition and venue settings × 2 weight sets = 48, each with 300 posterior draws.
    • 
      

  

  
Hierarchical Bayesian model

    
Value on each ball is modelled with a player effect, a player-by-season effect, an opposition effect and a venue effect, plus format, innings and role terms. Partial pooling shrinks small samples toward the mean. Gibbs sampling runs 2,000 iterations (600 burn-in) for the reported fit and 1,300 (450) for the 48 specifications. Estimated between-player, season, opposition and venue standard deviations per ball are 0.046, 0.039, 0.034 and 0.040 for batters and 0.045, 0.046, 0.078 and 0.056 for bowlers.

Limitations

    
    
      
  • Weak persistence. Pressure performance is only weakly predictive. The report describes historical performance with uncertainty and makes no forecast.
    • 
      
  • Pressure-specific ability is hard to separate from general ability, and the vs-usual comparison has almost no signal.
    • 
      
  • First-innings win probability is weak (AUC 0.667), so first-innings PI is less certain.
    • 
      
  • Definition and assumption dependence. Lists change with the pressure definition and wicket value, so results are averaged over 48 specifications.
    • 
      
  • Model simplifications. Gaussian noise that scales with balls, fixed-effect covariates, and opposition strength measured as a team effect.
    • 
      
  • Estimated scores and missing detail. Extras are partly recorded, dismissal types are blank and 16 matches have no ball-by-ball data. Fielding and partnerships are not modelled.
    • 
      
  • Non-IPL players such as Pakistani players appear in the ranking where the data warrants it but are not realistic IPL acquisitions.
    • 
      
  • No price, age, fitness or domestic data were supplied.
